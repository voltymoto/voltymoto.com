The U1 / Electric Utility Motorcycle

A work motorcycle, not a converted commuter

Designed around a working day: 200 kg of payload, racks and packs a rider can swap around in under two minutes, and mounting and hooking points that let the load sit where the job needs it.

The idea

What a work motorcycle means

In Vietnam, the majority of electric motorcycles available to delivery riders are simply standard commuter bikes fitted with aftermarket racks. Originally built for a single person's daily office commute, these vehicles are instead tasked with hauling a hundred kilograms of cargo over ten-hour shifts.

The U1 takes the exact opposite approach. Built entirely around the demands of a full work cycle, its frame structure, rack geometry, and battery layout were prioritized first, with styling shaped around those functional needs. This fundamentally different design philosophy yields practical advantages that go far beyond standard technical specifications.

  • 01Load is designed in, not added on. Mounting points exist at the front, the middle and the rear from the frame up.
  • 02Downtime is the real cost. Energy comes out by hand, so the bike is back on the road in under two minutes rather than off it for hours.
  • 03A rider's actual workday differs from a laboratory cycle. Because only one of these figures truly benefits the rider, we publish both the real-world loaded range and the theoretical maximum.

Energy

Swap in under two minutes

You can use a single 2.2 kWh battery pack for shorter routes to keep the bike lightweight, or install two packs for longer shifts. Because both packs are easily removable by hand, you can take a battery upstairs to charge in an apartment while leaving the bike parked on the street.

Battery swaps require less than two minutes. Furthermore, because the U1 is not locked into a proprietary swapping network, operators retain full freedom to choose their preferred energy provider.

Load where the job needs it

Designed for the demands of daily delivery, the bike handles up to 200 kg of payload with flexible mounting points located at the front, middle, and rear. Because different cargo, such as food bags, parcel cages, or fuel-style courier boxes, requires distinct placement, the frame offers versatile mounting configurations rather than forcing a single position.

To support hundreds of loaded stop-and-go starts each day, it delivers 152 Nm of torque at the wheel for effortless pull-away acceleration at traffic lights.

Range with and without loads

While 195 km represents the absolute maximum range, real-world conditions with two battery packs and a full load through city traffic yield between 140 and 160 km. We share both figures because shift operators require realistic performance data, making a specification sheet that features only maximum figures impractical.

Additionally, the vehicle reaches a top speed of 80 km/h, which easily exceeds the legal speed limits across all delivery and ride-hailing routes in Ho Chi Minh City.
