Scenario

Swap timings reflect the design targets for the U1 bay: 54 seconds for a single pack at a cabinet, 1 minute 58 seconds for both. Home charging uses the same removable pack in a floor dock from a standard household outlet, at roughly 2.5 hours per pack. The pack format is common to the cabinets of our launch partner network, and the bay is engineered to accept packs from more than one provider. Selex Smart Electric Vehicle JSC is our lead battery-swap partner at launch.

Why It Is Different

No single network owns this bike.

Most electric two-wheelers in this market tie the rider to one battery brand. When that vendor sets a price, closes a station, or leaves a district uncovered, the rider absorbs it. The U1 bay was engineered the other way around.

What You Are Riding

Built to be fixed, loaded, and lived with.

Four things a rider notices in the first week, and keeps noticing in year three.

Running gear

Parts a street mechanic already stocks

Chain final drive, twin rear shocks, a conventional swingarm, and disc brakes at both ends. None of that is exotic, and that is the point.

A belt drive or a hub motor would tidy the picture and put the rider at the mercy of one supplier. Everything wearing on this bike is a part a corner-shop mechanic in Vietnam can source and fit today, on a Sunday, without an appointment.

The exposed chain will need adjusting and the pads will need changing. Both take minutes and cost very little, which is the trade we chose over sealed components that fail rarely and expensively.

Load path

The racks are structure, not accessories

Front deck, mid rails, and rear deck all bolt to the frame independently, not to each other. Take one off and the others do not move, and neither does the way the bike carries a load.

The rated payload is 200 kg, and that number is a frame number, not a rack number. It is what the structure is designed to carry once the racks are hung on it.

For a fleet, that means one chassis and a parts list, rather than a different vehicle for every job. Reconfiguring a bike between a parcel round and a passenger shift is a spanner and ten minutes.

Cockpit

Everything a working rider touches, and nothing else

Wide bars, a flat single-piece seat, and one instrument pod. There is no second screen, no menu tree, and no app required to move the bike.

Twelve hours is the design case, not a one hour test ride. Bar height and reach, seat length, and peg position were set for a rider who is still on the bike at hour eleven with a full load behind them.

The seat takes two people. A working bike that cannot carry a passenger on a Sunday is a bike a rider will replace as soon as they can afford to.

Presence

Seen before dawn and after dusk

Full ring LED at the front, LED at the rear. Delivery and ride-hailing run in the dark at both ends of the day, and being seen early is a safety feature rather than a styling one.

LED units last the life of the vehicle, which removes a recurring cost a rider otherwise pays out of their own pocket, and removes a reason to be off the road.

Configure It

Same bike. Four working days.

Nothing structural changes between these. The frame, the bay, and the mounting points are identical, and the rest is a parts list.

Stripped to what a passenger shift needs

Single pack, front deck only, lowest weight. This is the configuration for dense inner-city work, where the bike spends its day threading traffic and what you want under you is light and narrow.
