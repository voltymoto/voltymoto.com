Before You Ask

How this process works.

The questions we get about the list itself: what it commits you to, and what happens after you send the form.

Does joining the list cost anything?
No. There is no payment on this website, and no deposit is taken at any point in the reservation process. Joining the list records your interest and your place in the queue for the first customer batch. If and when a deposit ever becomes part of the process, you will be asked separately and in writing, with terms in front of you first.
What happens after I submit the form?
The form lands directly in our order book. We confirm receipt, and we contact you when there is something real to tell you: the release date, the final specification, or a question about your configuration. We do not send marketing for the sake of it.
I run a fleet. Is the process different?
Yes. Fleet enquiries go to the same form, but tell us your fleet size and districts and we route you to a pilot conversation instead of the retail queue. A pilot is 10 to 100 units on your own routes with your own riders, and an operating report at the end of it.
Can I change my configuration later?
Yes. Nothing on the list is binding. Configuration is confirmed with you directly before any vehicle is built, and the U1 reconfigures with a spanner afterwards anyway.
Which language will you contact me in?
Whichever you prefer. Our team works in Vietnamese and English, and you can note your preference on the form.
FAQ

Questions about the vehicle.

Range, price, charging, load, servicing, and the things riders ask before they commit to a bike.

When can I actually get one?
The U1 is a running prototype in ride testing. No vehicle can be delivered to a customer until whole-vehicle type approval is granted by the Vietnam Register, and we will not publish a delivery date we cannot hold. Everyone on the list hears the date at the same time, before it is announced publicly.
How far does it actually go on a charge?
Up to 195 km on two packs. Fully loaded, in real traffic, plan on 140 to 160 km. A single pack halves that and suits dense inner-city work. Real range moves with speed, load, road, and weather, like any vehicle.
How much will it cost?
Indicative launch pricing is ₫34,190,000. That figure is indicative, not final: pricing is confirmed with each reservation holder directly, and fleet pricing is set per agreement. Our Rider Economics page shows what the running costs do to your monthly take home at your own distance.
What does the battery cost to run?
The packs are rented rather than bought with the vehicle. Current partner pricing on the unlimited-distance self-charge plans is ₫918,000 a month for three packs (TP3U) or ₫1,188,000 for four (TP4U), VAT included. Swapping at a station is billed separately at ₫8,000 per battery, and charging a pack at home costs roughly ₫6,600 in electricity. A refundable ₫400,000 deposit applies per battery. Battery wear is the network operator’s problem, not yours, and plan terms can change at renewal.
Can I charge at home instead of swapping?
Yes. Take a pack out and put it in a floor dock plugged into a standard household outlet. Roughly 2.5 hours per pack, no wallbox and no installation. Most working riders will do both: swap on route, charge overnight.
What happens if a swap cabinet is not near my route?
Check coverage for your district before you commit. The bay is engineered to accept packs from more than one provider, so coverage grows as networks are added, and home charging covers the gap in the meantime.
How much can it carry?
The frame is rated for 200 kg. That is a structural number, not a rack number: front deck, mid rails, and rear deck each bolt to the frame independently, so you can reconfigure between a parcel round and a passenger shift with a spanner.
Who services it, and how often?
Any competent mechanic. Chain drive, twin rear shocks, conventional swingarm, disc brakes: everything that wears is a part a corner-shop can source and fit. Expect two to three service stops a year, brakes and tires, against four to six on a petrol bike.
What licence do I need to ride it?
The U1 tops out at 80 km/h, which places it in a licensed motorcycle class rather than the moped category. Final classification is confirmed at type approval, and we will publish it then. Check your own licence class against the approved figures before you buy.
Can it carry a passenger?
Yes. The seat takes two people. A working bike that cannot carry someone on a Sunday is a bike a rider replaces as soon as they can afford to.
Is there a warranty?
Warranty terms are being finalized alongside type approval and will be published in full before any customer delivery, not after. You will see them before you are asked to pay anything.
